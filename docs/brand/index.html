branding

Wordmark: "O"xbot, a round O as tall as the b, with long red horns, then xbot in Roboto Mono Bold with a slightly thicker outline, no background (wordmark.svg, wordmark-dark.svg, wordmark-current.svg; letters are outlines, so no font is needed). It goes in the app sidebar, the website and the setup guide. App icon: the same O with red horns, white ring on a black tile (logo-O-ink.svg; menu bar: logo-O-template.svg). The options below are the earlier icon explorations. Logo is two horns; color comes from The AI Switch (ink, paper, one Switch red). Every image here is SVG. Files are in docs/brand/svg/.

2 Per-chat avatars

Twelve little oxen, one per bot. Each has its own body shape, horn style, expression and color. Same recipe across all: solid body, horns in the body color, white eyes.

Assignment rule: hash the bot id (FNV-1a) → shape = hash % 12, color = (hash / 12) % 12. Same bot, same look, forever. Not user-pickable. With 12 × 12 combos, two bots rarely look alike.

Avatar colors

All mid-tone so they hold up on white and on #181C22. Brand red is left out on purpose; it stays for buttons. White eyes on every color.

3 UI palette

Tokens for the app. Minimal: white backgrounds, white buttons with red writing and a thin gray edge. No red outlines on text boxes or selected items; focus and selection are gray. "New bot" is a round + next to the wordmark. Your chat bubbles are black with white text. Approval cards use amber, not red.

Danger / approval color: amber (recommended). The app asks for your OK before risky actions. If those cards were red, they'd look like the Send button and the brand, and "red" would mean both "go" and "stop." Amber says "look first" without competing with the brand. Red stays for the logo horns and the button labels.

Contrast note: button text uses a slightly deeper red, #E02424 on white (4.6 : 1), so labels pass AA; the logo keeps #FF3737. Your bubbles are white on black (18 : 1). Red stays out of body text.
