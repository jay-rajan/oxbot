Bot settings: what it's for first, permissions optional

Today the window shows the name, who it's shared with, a hidden "Kind of job", the always-allowed list, reports and schedules. The bot's purpose (its instructions) can't be seen or changed after it's made. This puts the purpose at the top and gathers the controls under one optional Permissions section, with safe defaults so most people never open it.

Order: who the bot is (name, purpose, who uses it), then what it does on its own (schedules, reports), then limits, folded away.

Permissions starts folded, with one line saying what's set: "Any website · asks before buying, sending, posting, signing in". It opens by itself when something non-default is set.

Who can change what: the person who manages the bot (its maker, or the owner) edits everything. Others see the purpose and permissions read-only, so they know what the bot does. Kids see the purpose only.

Buttons stay visible at the bottom while the window scrolls, as in Settings.

Gone from the top level: "Kind of job" moves into Advanced (it already was), and nothing else is removed.

3 · Building it

  • Protocol: SetBotInstructions {bot, text} and SetBotSites {bot, sites | any}, each checked on the server (manage rights, not a kid, not from untrusted content).
  • Bot file: bot.toml gains sites = ["loblaws.ca"] (absent = any). Instructions are written all at once (temp file, then rename), like bot.toml today.
  • Hub and gate: the site check goes in the gate before every page visit, alongside the existing checks (home network, sign-ins). Changed instructions close the AI session so the next turn starts with the new purpose.
  • Screen: the window above, in the current page (and the same pieces in React later).
  • Tests: unit tests for the site check, edit rights and atomic writes; end-to-end: edit the purpose, limit sites and see a refusal, read-only view for another person, small-program switch.

4 · Decisions for you

  1. Website limit: include "Only these websites" (recommended), or keep permissions to what exists today?
  2. Editing the purpose: start the AI fresh on change (recommended), or keep its memory and only add the new purpose?
  3. Others' view: show the purpose and permissions read-only to everyone the bot is shared with (recommended), or hide them?
