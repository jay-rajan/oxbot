Simpler settings: four tabs down the side, one fixed size

Settings goes from seven tabs to four, listed down the left side. The window keeps one size whatever is open, and anything long scrolls inside it. AI models and Schedules leave Settings. Devices joins People. Activity shows everything that left the home computer.

The window is one fixed size (about 760 × 560). Only the right side scrolls, so switching tabs never resizes it. Below 600px wide, the tabs become a row across the top.

3 · AI without the AI models tab

  • Models per bot are picked automatically. When a bot is made, oxbot reads its instructions and picks the kind of job (lookup, research, shopping, writing…). That picks the ordered model list we already have, free first. The bot settings lose the AI models box.
  • Paid models are on by default, within the monthly limit ($10 for your own account, the trial's $2, or the paid plan's key). The owner changes the limit with one field under General → AI, which shows only when the account has credit.
  • Personal details are always removed before anything is sent, so private bots can use free models too. The switch goes away, and so does "Ask AI companies not to keep what's sent".
  • Daily question limits stay at their defaults with no fields. A bot that hits one says so in the chat.
  • Shadow is removed (Nov 7): the Shadow button in the message bar, its dialog, and the side-by-side answers it adds to the chat.
  • Hidden as developer tools: Compare, the comparison settings and the usage table. They're switched on with a developer flag, not shown to homes.
  • The "AI helper" section is gone from view. If it's missing, Setup's line at the top of General says so.

4 · Activity: what left the home computer

  • One row for each exchange with the outside: AI (each question to OpenRouter: bot, model, sent and received sizes, cost, personal details swapped), Websites (each page visit by a bot: the site and the bytes in and out, with third-party hosts folded under it), Devices (phones connecting), and oxbot (its own calls: the OpenRouter sign-in, the free models list, update checks).
  • Filter chips: All, AI, Websites, Devices, oxbot. Newest first, with the day's totals at the top.
  • Who sees what: the owner sees the whole home. Everyone else sees their own bots and devices. Kid accounts see nothing (their parent does).
  • Tap a row for details: the host, the time it took, and the outcome. For an AI row: the model that answered, tokens, and which kinds of personal detail were swapped (for example "2 emails, 1 name"), never the values.
  • Kept for 30 days, on the home computer only, then deleted.
  • Built from: AI rows come from the model_calls table, which already exists. Website rows come from the browser's network events per page, added up per site. Device and oxbot rows come from the server. All of them go into one new net_log table.

Not shown: the text of what was sent. Keeping it would mean storing every question and page a bot read. That's another copy of private data to protect, so it's left out unless you want it (decision 3).

5 · Notifications

  • One switch: "Tell me when AI use passes 50% and 80% of a limit." The list of limits (free questions, paid spending, account credit, each bot's questions) moves out of the label.
  • "Waiting for my OK or a sign-in while oxbot is hidden" becomes automatic, with no switch. Mac notifications still need permission in System Settings. If permission is off, General shows one line saying so.
  • The Browser section is removed. "Sign out of all sites" moves to the bottom of Websites.
