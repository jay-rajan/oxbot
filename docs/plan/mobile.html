oxbot on your Pixel

The Android app is a device: a window onto the home computer Mac. It runs no bots and can never be the home computer.

Waiting for your OK. Nothing is installed or built yet. This also moves Android ahead of iPhone in the roadmap (#8 said iPhone first).

What you'll have at the end

  • An oxbot app on your Pixel, installed over a USB cable. No Play Store and no Google developer account.
  • You open Settings on the Mac, tap Add a device, choose who the phone is for (Jay), and scan the QR code with the phone. That's done once.
  • From then on, the phone shows your bots on home Wi-Fi: chat, see their status, approve red cards, and see others' private bots as locked.
  • Signing in to a site from the phone: the home computer's sign-in window shows live on the phone, and your taps and typing go to your own Chrome on the home computer.

Rules it keeps

Phone is never the home computerAndroid builds have no server, no bots, no Chrome. Only the device part is compiled in.
Data goes phone ↔ home computer onlyDirectly over home Wi-Fi, encrypted. No relay, no cloud, no Google push service, telemetry off.
The home computer keeps its powersEven your own phone can't do Home-computer-only things (delete others' bots, see others' private chats).
Same page as the MacOne UI, made to fit a phone. The phone loads it from the home computer, so the two never fall out of step.

Design choices and why

ChoiceWhy
One oxbot app (Tauri 2), with an Android build that only has the deviceOne codebase and one page. The Mac-only parts (server, tray, Chrome) are left out of the Android build, so a phone can't be the home computer.
The page reaches the home computer through the app's Rust part, not straight from the web viewA web view can't pin a home-made certificate, and it shouldn't hold the device key. The phone opens no ports, so other apps on it can't reach the home computer.
The home computer picks the person when it makes the QR codeA phone can't claim to be someone else. The code works once and expires after 10 minutes, with only a few tries allowed.
Home computer certificate made on the Mac, its fingerprint inside the QREncryption without any outside certificate authority. A fake home computer on the Wi-Fi is refused.
Device key in the Android Keystore; the home computer stores only a hash of itA copied database can't be used to pose as your phone. Settings → Devices lists each phone with Remove.
Finds the home computer by its last address, then by Wi-Fi discovery (mDNS) if the Mac's address changedRouters move addresses around; you shouldn't have to pair again.
Phone layout: bot list → chat with a back button; long-press for the bot menu; Settings full screenThe Mac's sidebar doesn't fit a phone. It's the same page, so the Mac layout is unchanged.
People get real ids (taken from #1)A device is tied to a person, not a display name. Needed for private bots to stay private.

Steps

  1. Set up Android tools on your Mac

    Install Java 17, the Android SDK, the NDK and adb through Homebrew (about 5 GB, no Android Studio needed), plus Rust's Android target. You turn on USB debugging on the Pixel.

    Check: an empty oxbot screen opens on the Pixel from cargo tauri android dev.

  2. People and devices on the home computer

    Real person ids, a devices table, and Settings → Devices: Add a device (choose the person, show the QR code), a list of paired phones, Remove.

    Tests: the code is single-use and expires; a removed phone is refused; only the Mac's own login is the home computer.

  3. The device door and the device link

    The home computer's encrypted door on home Wi-Fi (its own certificate, pairing, device keys, discovery). The oxbot-device crate on the other side.

    Tests: pair, chat as a person, wrong certificate refused, private bots locked for others, Home-computer-only actions refused. /pr_test gets a stand-in device.

  4. The Android app

    The Device-only Android build, QR scanning, Keystore storage, and loading the page from the home computer.

    Check on your Pixel: scan, pair, chat with a bot, approve a red card. I can take screenshots of the phone over the cable to check each screen.

  5. Phone layout

    The page fits a phone: list and chat screens, long-press menu, large tap targets, the phone's safe areas, the keyboard not hiding the message box.

    Tests: /pr_test runs the main tests again at phone size (Pixel's 412 px width).

  6. Signing in from the phone

    The home computer streams the sign-in window from your Chrome to the phone; your taps and typing go back. The bot is paused the whole time and never sees it.

    Check: sign in to a test site from the Pixel, then a private bot uses that sign-in.

What I need from you

  • OK on this plan and the ~5 GB tool install.
  • On the Pixel: Settings → About phone → tap Build number 7 times, then Settings → System → Developer options → USB debugging on. Plug it into the Mac and tap Allow.
  • When macOS asks whether oxbot may accept incoming connections, click Allow (the device door).

Not in this plan

  • Using it away from home. It needs a relay or VPN, which the data rule rules out. A separate decision later.
  • Alerts while the app is closed. Android needs Google's push service for that; alerts show while the app is open (with #5).
  • Files and voice from the phone: come with #3 (voice uses the Pixel's on-device speech).
  • iPhone and the Play Store: later, with #8 and #9.
