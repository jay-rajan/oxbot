oxbot · plan · how bots search and browse · Nov 10, 2026

Tune how bots look things up, on 150 household tasks

The goal is one general way of working that fits every lookup, from "best TV deals" to "is the library open Sunday". No rules for one task or one site. Each batch of 10 tasks is measured, the bots' rules are changed, and the next batch checks whether the change holds.

1. The tasks

150 household tasks for a family, written as people actually ask. I write them, and you review the list before anything runs. Each task has a few checks instead of a fixed answer, since answers change week to week. Example checks: "has links", "prices are current", "mentions this year".

120 tasks are used for tuning. 30 are held back and run only at the start and the end, to show the rules got better in general and not just on the tasks they were tuned on.

2. How each task runs

  • As a practice run. This is oxbot's sandbox: its own bot and folder, a separate Chrome with no sign-ins, and a network guard that stops anything being sent or changed. Your bots and your browser are untouched.
  • Anything that needs a person is skipped. Approval requests, sign-ins and "are you a person" checks are noted, never shown, and the bot carries on. Those counts are part of the result.
  • Same conditions as your bots: the same rules, the same pacing per site, and today's date on every message.

3. What is measured

MeasureHow
Did it answer well?A grading model reads the task, its checks, the reply, and the pages the bot opened, and scores 0–3. I spot-check the grading by hand.
StepsModel requests and browser steps per task; whether it hit the limit.
How it went about itWhether it started with a web search, how many sites it opened, guessed addresses (pages not found), pages opened twice, items looked up one by one, whether it read a page again for no reason, and whether it needed a person.
LinksWhether it cited pages it actually opened, and whether those pages are current.

4. The tuning loop

  • Only general rules. A change can't name a site, a store or a kind of task. "Search the web first, then open the best two or three results" is fine. "Use Rotten Tomatoes for movies" isn't.
  • Rules stay short. They are capped in length, so tuning can't keep stacking rules on top of each other.
  • Every change is logged, with its reason and before/after scores. You can undo any of them.

5. What you get

  • After each pass, a report page: scores, steps, what changed, and the pattern the bots are settling into, in plain words. For example: "search the web → open 2 results → read only the part that answers → reply with links, about 6 steps".
  • The rule changes, staged and not committed, to review.
  • The task set and its runner kept in the repo, so later changes can be checked against the same 150 tasks.

Decisions

1. Model and budget. My recommendation is the same order your bots use (free first, then cheap paid), with a hard cap of $40. I stop and ask before going past it.
2. Grading model. A stronger model than the one being tuned grades. It's a few cents per task, and I recommend it.
3. Three runs at once on different sites to finish faster: yes (recommended), or one at a time?
4. The task list. I write the 150 and you review them before the first run. Add any of your own, like "best TV deals".
