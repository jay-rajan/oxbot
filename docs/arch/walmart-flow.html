oxbot Walmart Flow

You type "Add the milk I bought last time on Walmart to my cart" to a shared bot that has never run. This follows that one message through every layer, 80 steps, until the reply is on your screen. Every blue chip opens that exact line on GitHub.

The story in one breath. The page sends ClientMsg::Send; the server hands it to the Hub; the Hub stores it, takes a turn slot and starts the bot's Codex process; Codex runs the loop, asking OpenRouter through oxbot's relay at every step; each tool Codex wants comes back to oxbot, where the gate asks the Hub's policy first. Opening and reading Walmart run straight away; the sign-in is done by you in a real Chrome window; "Add to cart" waits for your Allow. The reply is checked, stored, and streamed to the page; then everything is released.

Things the trace turned up

  • No queue. A second message to a busy bot is refused (HubError::Busy), not queued.
  • Your message isn't drawn until it's stored. The page waits for the server's echo, so what you see is always what's in oxbot.db.
  • Streaming text isn't stored. TextDelta is broadcast only; the final AssistantMessage is what's kept. The meter line is also broadcast only.
  • The sign-in cookie check doesn't reach the bot. Pressing "I'm signed in" always tells the bot the window finished; the cookie check only decides whether Walmart is listed under Sign-ins.
  • The bot holds the browser through your sign-in. Other bots on the same Chrome wait the whole time (up to 10 minutes).
  • The 20-minute cap pauses during tool calls, so waiting on an approval request doesn't use it up.
  • chrome-devtools-mcp often starts at session open, to list its tools, unless the tool list is already cached.
